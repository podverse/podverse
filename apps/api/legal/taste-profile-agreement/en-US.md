# How a Listen Is Counted

A listen counts when you have heard 60 seconds, or 25% of the episode or track, whichever comes first. Each counted listen is its own record: the episode or track, the clip when you played a clip, and the show it belongs to. Hearing it again can count again. A new listen that arrives too soon after your last recorded one is not recorded. There is a daily limit on how many listens are recorded. A lower daily limit sets how many of them count toward the public lists; listens past it still update your Taste Profile. Listening that looks automated can be left out of the public lists; it still updates your Taste Profile.

On the website, two more things are recorded for the public lists only: opening someone else's profile, and opening a playlist. Those opens do not update your Taste Profile. There is a daily limit on how many of those opens are recorded. Opening your own profile is not recorded. The mobile apps do not record those opens.

Listening while signed out is not counted. If Taste Profile is off, new listens are not recorded.

# What We Store

Each listen record has a random id for your account, which thing you played, how many seconds you heard, whether you finished it, and when. An open on the website records the id, what you opened, and when. If listening looks automated, the id also gets a mark with the reason and the time. While the mark is in place, the id's listens and opens are left out of the public lists. Listens and opens recorded while the mark was in place stay left out after it is removed. We also keep a count of recent minutes in which a new listen arrived too soon, and when the last one was. The mark and that count stay with the id when you turn Taste Profile off, and are deleted when you delete your account. A record does not include your name, your email, or your username. That id stays with your account. It is not shown on your public profile.

We can still connect the id back to your account. That is how turning this off, and deleting your account, applies to you. This is not anonymous. We do not give the id to anyone else.

# Your Taste Profile

Your Taste Profile is a row for each show you listen to: a podcast, an album, or an artist. Episodes and tracks are not their own rows. A listen to an episode, a track, or a clip updates the show it belongs to. The row keeps how many times you have listened, how many you finished, and when you first and last listened. It also keeps a picture of what you have been listening to lately and what you have listened to over a longer time.

You can see your Taste Profile in Settings. You can remove a show from it. That show moves to Not Counted in Recommendations, and its taste data is deleted. Later listens to a show on that list still count on the public lists, and they do not add the show back.

You can see Not Counted in Recommendations. Taking a show off that list asks you to choose: add it back to your Taste Profile, starting over, or only take it off the list. If you only take it off the list, a later listen can add it again.

Opening a profile or a playlist does not update your Taste Profile.

# What the Lists Show

The public lists show counts. They do not show who listened. Those counts are not tied to your account. Each list counts you at most once per episode, show, clip, profile, or playlist. The day, week, and month lists are counted from stored records, so when your records are deleted, your listening leaves those lists. The all-time count adds you again only after a set period.

# What We Do Not Do

- We do not sell this information.
- We do not share your Taste Profile with anyone else.
- We do not use it to advertise to you.

# How Long We Keep It

Listen records are kept for {retention_days} days. We then delete a whole month of records at once, so a record can remain until its month is removed. Your Taste Profile is not on that schedule. It stays until you turn Taste Profile off or you delete your account.

All-time counts already added to the public lists stay after a record is deleted. Those counts are numbers, not a list of listeners.

Deleting your account deletes the random id, your listen records, your Taste Profile, and your Not Counted in Recommendations list. The public all-time counts stay.

# Changing Your Mind

Turn this on or off in Settings. Turning it off asks you to confirm, because we then delete your Taste Profile, your Not Counted in Recommendations list, and your stored listen records right away. All-time counts already on the public lists stay. If you turn it on later, recommendations start from scratch. If you turn it on again, the daily limits do not start over, and for a set period after you turned it off, your new listens update your Taste Profile but do not count toward the public lists, and opening a profile or a playlist is not recorded.

If your membership ends, or if this explanation changes and you have not confirmed it yet, we stop new records and keep your Taste Profile paused. Your Not Counted in Recommendations list stays. Listen records already stored still age out on the schedule above. The Taste Profile starts updating again when tracking is on. We do not delete it in those cases.

If you turn it off, we will not ask again when this explanation changes. If you turn it on, and we later change what we record or how long we keep it, we will ask again. Until you say yes to that update, tracking stays off.
